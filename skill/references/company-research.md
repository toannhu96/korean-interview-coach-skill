# Company Research

Use this file when preparing a candidate for a named Korean company, Korean-owned company, Korean client-facing role, or Korea-facing team.

## Goal

Turn company research into interview strategy. Do not create a generic company summary. Every finding should affect how the candidate answers, what stories they choose, or what questions they ask.

## Source Tiers

Label company claims clearly:

- **Verified**: Company website, job description, recruiter message, official newsroom, product docs, investor relations, public filings, or direct candidate-provided material.
- **Likely**: Credible recent news, reputable industry sources, repeated public employee/interview reports, or consistent patterns across multiple sources.
- **Unknown**: Unverified culture claims, stale posts, anonymous comments, assumptions about Korean companies in general, or anything not checked.

Never present `Likely` or `Unknown` claims as fact.

## Research Checklist

For a named company, collect only what helps interview prep:

1. **Business and product**: What the company sells, to whom, and what seems strategically important now.
2. **Role fit**: What the JD repeats, what outcomes the role likely owns, and which skills matter most.
3. **Korean-market context**: HQ relationship, local office role, cross-border collaboration, clients, or Korean-language/stakeholder exposure.
4. **Culture and working style**: Only use verified or carefully labeled evidence. Look for teamwork, speed, hierarchy, quality bar, customer focus, and execution style.
5. **Interview process clues**: Recruiter notes, candidate-provided schedule, public interview reports, or role-specific format hints.
6. **Candidate risks**: English clarity, foreign-worker context, visa/relocation, local market knowledge, seniority mismatch, or missing Korean-company experience.
7. **Candidate advantages**: Vietnam market knowledge, cross-cultural experience, technical/role expertise, reliability, language ability, or domain fit.

## Interview Strategy Rules

- If the company is a large Korean conglomerate, emphasize reliability, coordination, quality, and ability to work within structured processes.
- If the company is a Korean startup or tech scaleup, emphasize adaptability, ownership, speed, and practical problem solving.
- If the role connects Korea and Vietnam, emphasize cross-cultural communication, local market understanding, and stakeholder translation.
- If the candidate lacks Korean-company experience, do not hide it. Reframe with learning speed, respect for different work styles, and evidence of adapting across teams.
- If English is the interview language, optimize for simple, clear English rather than native-like fluency.

## Unknown Handling

Say this when company-specific research is missing:

```text
Mình chưa có đủ nguồn xác minh về công ty này, nên phần dưới đây là prep theo JD/context bạn đưa và kỳ vọng chung của công ty Hàn. Mình sẽ không giả định văn hoá công ty nếu chưa có nguồn.
```

Then give general Korean-company guidance and ask for the JD, recruiter email, company page, or interview schedule.

## Anti-Patterns

Avoid:

- Claiming all Korean companies work the same way.
- Using anonymous culture comments as fact.
- Overloading the candidate with company history.
- Turning research into trivia that does not improve answers.
- Inventing interview formats.
- Coaching candidates to use Korean phrases unless they are accurate and relevant.
