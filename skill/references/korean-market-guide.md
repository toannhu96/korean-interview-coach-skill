# Korean Market Guide

Use this guide to calibrate interview feedback for Korean companies and Korea-facing teams. Treat these as general patterns, not universal rules. If the candidate provides company-specific information, prefer that.

## What Korean Companies Often Value

- **Reliability**: Follow-through, punctuality, consistency, and not creating avoidable risk for the team.
- **Team fit**: Respectful collaboration, awareness of group goals, and willingness to coordinate before acting alone.
- **Ownership with humility**: Clear individual contribution without sounding self-centered.
- **Learning attitude**: Ability to receive feedback, adapt quickly, and improve after mistakes.
- **Seniority awareness**: Respect for managers, leads, clients, and decision makers.
- **Preparation**: Understanding the company, product, role, and why Korea or a Korean company is a serious target.
- **Communication clarity**: Short, structured English that interviewers can follow even if English is not their first language.

## Korean-Company Answer Standard

A strong answer usually shows:

1. The candidate understood the business or team context.
2. The candidate acted responsibly, not impulsively.
3. The candidate coordinated with stakeholders.
4. The candidate can explain their direct contribution.
5. The candidate learned something practical.
6. The candidate connects the story to the target company or role.

## Common Risks for Vietnamese and Foreign Candidates

- Giving a long background before answering the question.
- Being too modest and hiding the candidate's real contribution.
- Saying "we" so often that the interviewer cannot see the candidate's ownership.
- Overcorrecting into aggressive self-promotion.
- Giving generic answers about "hard work" or "passion" without evidence.
- Not explaining why they want a Korean company specifically.
- Treating Korean companies as interchangeable instead of showing company-level preparation.
- Missing hierarchy or stakeholder context in conflict stories.
- Using complex English when simpler English would sound more confident.

## Communication Calibration

Prefer:

- "My role was..."
- "I aligned with my manager/team before..."
- "The tradeoff was..."
- "I learned that..."
- "For this role, that matters because..."

Avoid:

- "I did everything by myself."
- "My previous company did not understand..."
- "I just worked hard."
- "Korea is my dream" without a concrete professional reason.
- Overusing Korean phrases to impress. One correct phrase is better than many awkward phrases.

## Useful Korean Context

Use Korean terms only when relevant:

- `선배/후배` (seonbae/hubae): senior/junior relationship context. Do not force this into answers.
- `팀워크` (teamwork): teamwork and coordination.
- `책임감` (sense of responsibility): reliability and ownership.
- `성실함` (diligence/sincerity): consistent effort and trustworthiness.
- `조직문화` (organizational culture): company culture.

Do not coach candidates to perform Korean identity. Coach them to communicate professionally in a way Korean interviewers can trust.

## Role-Specific Signals

### Engineering

- Technical clarity, debugging process, ownership of production issues, collaboration with PM/design/QA, risk management.

### Product / Project Management

- Stakeholder alignment, prioritization, customer understanding, metrics, tradeoffs, execution discipline.

### Sales / Business Development

- Trust-building, market understanding, follow-up discipline, negotiation awareness, relationship management.

### Operations / Support

- Reliability, escalation judgment, process improvement, customer empathy, detail orientation.

### Design / Marketing / Content

- Audience understanding, brand fit, rationale behind decisions, iteration from feedback, measurable impact.

## Etiquette Notes

- Be on time and prepared.
- Start with a polite greeting and direct answer.
- Keep answers concise unless asked for detail.
- Do not criticize previous employers harshly.
- When discussing conflict, show respect and resolution.
- When discussing mistakes, show ownership and learning.
- Ask thoughtful questions about team expectations, onboarding, collaboration style, and success metrics.
